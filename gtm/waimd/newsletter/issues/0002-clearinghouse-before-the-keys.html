The drug & alcohol program has to exist before the first dispatch

Before any driver performs a safety-sensitive function, FMCSA expects a working drug & alcohol testing program and Clearinghouse registration already in place. This is one of the first things a Safety Audit checks — and "we were getting to it" is exactly the gap auditors are trained to find.

 

KEY TAKEAWAYS

1 The program comes before the driver, not after. A DOT drug & alcohol testing program must be operational *before* anyone performs a safety-sensitive function — including driving a CMV.
2 Most small carriers join a consortium (C/TPA). A consortium/third-party administrator runs your random testing pool and recordkeeping so a one- or two-truck carrier can meet the same federal standard.
3 Register in the FMCSA Clearinghouse. Carriers must register and query the Clearinghouse for drug & alcohol violations before a driver first operates, and at least once a year for current drivers.
4 Pre-employment isn't optional. A pre-employment drug test (and a Clearinghouse query) is generally required before a new driver's first safety-sensitive function.
5 Keep the proof. Auditors want to see the program, the testing records, and your Clearinghouse queries — not just your word that a pool exists.

▸ DO THIS NOW

If you haven't registered in the FMCSA Clearinghouse and joined a consortium/C-TPA, treat that as your top priority this week — before another driver turns a key.
