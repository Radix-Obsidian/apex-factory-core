dek: >
  When the Safety Audit starts, the Driver Qualification File is often the first thing on the table.
  It's a per-driver folder that proves each person behind the wheel is legally qualified — and a
  thin or missing file is one of the fastest ways to rack up violations.
date: June 25, 2026
issueNo: 3
slug: driver-qualification-files
ctaLead: The first folder they open should already be complete. Build the file before the audit, not during it.
takeaways:
  - "**One file per driver, kept current.** A DQF documents that each driver is qualified to operate, and you maintain it for as long as they drive for you (and for a period after they leave)."
  - "**The employment application + inquiries.** Expect the driver's application for employment, plus the required inquiries into their driving record and prior DOT-regulated employers."
  - "**The medical certificate.** A current medical examiner's certificate (and, where applicable, the MVR confirming CDL medical certification status) belongs in the file."
  - "**The road test or its equivalent.** A road-test certificate — or an accepted equivalent, like a valid CDL where permitted — shows the driver was actually evaluated."
  - "**The annual review.** Each year you review the driver's motor vehicle record and document a determination that they remain qualified."
doThisNow: Open one driver's file right now and check it against the list above. If any single piece is missing, you've just found exactly what an auditor would.
cfr:
  - 49 CFR 391.51
  - 49 CFR Part 391, Subpart E

DQFs reward boring discipline: a checklist, a folder, and a calendar reminder for the annual review.
That's the whole game. Build the habit before the audit and the "first folder on the table" becomes
your strongest one. Decision-support, not legal advice — verify the full file contents and retention
periods against FMCSA.
